📖 Background¶

You are a data analyst for a United Nations initiative focused on understanding global health trends. Your latest assignment is to explore and visualize life expectancy data from around the world, focusing on gender differences.

Life expectancy can vary significantly over time and across different countries due to numerous factors including advancements in medicine, a country's development level, and the impacts of conflicts. Interestingly, data consistently shows that women tend to live longer than men, raising intriguing questions. Could this be due to biological factors or perhaps because women generally care for their health better?

Your task is to explore these patterns and disparities.

💾 The Data¶

The dataset contains information about life expectancy in various countries or areas broken down by gender and time periods. The data is sourced from the United Nations Population Division, Gender Statistics, Life Expectancy at Birth.

Objectives¶

Analyze and visualize global life expectancy data with a focus on gender disparities, using a United Nations dataset. As part of the analysis, answer the following key questions: 1) Does the Value column contain any missing data?

2) How does life expectancy differ between men and women across countries overall in the 2000-2005 period?

3) Which countries exhibit the largest disparities in life expectancy between genders in the 2000-2005 subgroup?

Summary of Findings & Recommendations¶

This project examined information on life expectancies of people across the globe between 2000-2005. In particular, differences in life expectancies of females & males were evaluated overall & by country. Countries were also assessed depending on how often one gender had a higher life expectancy as well as where the most significant disparities were between genders.
Before then, the data was examined to look for inconsistencies, particularly missing values, that could inhibit such analyses.

Preprocessing (Analysis I)¶

The dataset, specifically the variable quantifying life expectancies, was assessed to look for missing values. Of the 1,560 data points in the dataset, none had a missing life expectancy. Moreover, there were no missing data points.

Analysis II - Life Expectancy by Gender¶

Firstly, life expectancies of males & females were evaluated overall during this time period to look for more general differences & trends that may be more indicative of biological or habitual differences.

Of the 195 countries with data points for both genders during this 2000-2005 period, the typical life expectancy of females & males was around 72 & 66 years respectively. Other statistical quantities in these distributions also corroborate this trend.
Additional analyses determined for how many countries one gender had a higher usual life expectancy as well as the average difference. 190 countries reported that females typically lived longer than males, whereas only one country reported the opposite. The average discrepancy in life expectancy of these 190 countries was nearly five years.

As such, it is evident that females typically lived longer than males across most of the globe during 2000-2005.

Analysis III - Differences in Life Expectancy by Gender, Country¶

Life expectancies by gender were evaluated further by incorporating specific countries. Such analyses can potentially help identify where living conditions or other circumstances might be the most impactful in regard to peoples' longevity.

Data was retrieved to find the countries in which the disparity in life expectancies between genders was the most significant during the 2000-2005 period. The five countries with the largest differences were the Russian Federation, Belarus, Estonia, Kazakhstan, & Lithuania, all of which saw a discrepancy of 12-14 years. Females typically lived longer than males for all five. Based on the usual life expectancies observed across the globe (see Analysis II), females from Kazakhstan were the only group of females, of these five countries, who lived to ages younger than this typical value (72). On the other hand, males of four of these five countries lived to ages less than this typical value (66).
As such, it appears that when life expectancies differ the most between males & females, males are slightly more affected in that they generally have less longevity than females.

Interestingly, these five countries are all in the region of Eastern Europe & North Asia. In fact, a rough circle or oval could be drawn on a map that encompasses these five countries whilst only including Latvia & part of Ukraine. Obviously, Russia is a very large country in terms of geographical area, but the fact that these five countries are within such a confined location indicates that this general region may be a relevant factor in the observed differences in life expectancy between males & females. Whether these differences are due more to living/environmental conditions or working conditions that are a part of daily life in these countries would require additional research.

Recommendations¶

Analyses came to quite common conclusions in that males typically lived shorter lives than females during the 2000-2005 period. Research could try to investigate what general differences exist between males & females that could lead to this, but the scope of such discrepancies is likely massive. From biological to sociological & cultural differences, various elements can cause females & males to behave & develop differently that can impact their longevity.
Alternatively, more effective research could try & investigate countries & areas in which discrepancies in life expectancies were the most severe. Doing so can more easily uncover trends or relevant factors that contribute to these observed differences. Based on Analysis III, countries in the area of Eastern Europe & North Asia exhibited the largest differences. Evaluating living conditions, working conditions, & other circumstances that could impact the health of people in this region could yield valuable insights.

Ultimately, such information could be valuable to organizations that aim to understand global health trends & find solutions in assisting less well-off areas that face more challenging conditions when it comes to their health. For example, such organizations could establish missions that aim to foster assistance & resources such as outreach programs or donation systems for the purposes of uplifting the living conditions of less developed or more impoverished peoples & areas.

#Next, find # countries in which females generally live longer than males & what the difference in life expectancy is
q2_data_I <- data %>%
    #filter for years of interest
    filter(Year == "2000-2005") %>%
    group_by(Country_Area, Subgroup) %>%
    ungroup() %>%
    #select columns of interest
    select(Country_Area, Subgroup, Value) %>%

    #For each country, want to indicate which Subgroup has the higher Value (life exp.), & by how much
    #first, change shape of data
    pivot_wider(names_from=Subgroup, values_from=Value) %>%
    #indicate when the Female life expectancy is >, <, or = to that of Males; obtain the absolute difference in life expectancy
    mutate(higher_LE = case_when(Female>Male ~ "Female", Male>Female ~ "Male", Female==Male ~ "Equal"), 
           abs_LE_diff = abs(Female-Male)) %>%
    #change shape of data into original structure
    pivot_longer(cols=Female:Male, names_to=c("Subgroup")) %>%
    #rename life expectancy column to original name
    rename(Value=value) %>%
    #reorganize the columns
    select(Country_Area, Subgroup, Value, higher_LE, abs_LE_diff)
